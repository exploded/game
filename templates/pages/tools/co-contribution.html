The most you could get, by income

How it works

You put after-tax money into your super fund, and your fund tells the ATO. Once you lodge your tax return, the ATO works out your co-contribution and pays it into your super. You don’t need to apply, but your money has to reach your fund by 30 June 2027 to count for 2026–27. It pays 50 cents for each dollar you put in, up to $500 for incomes up to $49,293. Above that, the most falls by about 3.3 cents for each extra dollar of income, to nothing at $64,293.

About this calculator

What it’s for

It estimates the government co-contribution you could get for 2026–27 on after-tax money you put into your super, and how much you’d need to put in to get the most for your income.

What it leaves out, and what that means

  • Your income is one figure. The calculator takes your total income as you type it. If you leave something out, such as reportable employer super, the estimate could be too high.
  • The ATO decides. The ATO works out the real amount from your tax return and what your fund reports. It may differ from this estimate.
  • Only this year. It doesn’t show what the money could grow to. The thresholds change each 1 July.
  • The conditions are yours to check. The calculator relies on the boxes you tick. The ATO’s page has the full rules, including which visa holders can get it.

Assumptions, and why we use them

Income thresholds $49,293 and $64,293 · fixed by law for 2026–27
Set by the government each year and indexed to wages. The full $500 is paid up to the lower threshold and nothing from the higher one up.
Rate and most paid 50 cents a dollar, up to $500 · fixed by law
Above the lower threshold the $500 falls by 3.333 cents for each dollar of income.
Rounding and least paid Up to the next 5 cents, at least $20 · fixed by law
As the ATO pays it: an amount under $20 is paid as $20.
