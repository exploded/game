About this calculator

What it’s for

The First Home Super Saver (FHSS) scheme lets you save for your first home inside super, where contributions are usually taxed at 15% instead of your marginal rate. This calculator estimates the deposit you could withdraw from extra before-tax contributions over up to 6 years, and compares it with saving the same take-home pay in a bank account. It’s an estimate to help you understand the scheme, not the amount the ATO will release.

How it works

  • It looks at before-tax contributions only: salary sacrifice, and personal contributions you claim a tax deduction for. Your employer’s compulsory super doesn’t count towards FHSS.
  • Your employer’s super is worked out on your salary before salary sacrifice, and counts towards the $32,500 before-tax cap.
  • Up to $15,000 a year and $50,000 in total can be counted. You can release 85% of these contributions, plus associated earnings. Amounts over a limit aren’t counted.
  • Associated earnings are estimated at the earnings rate, assuming each year’s contributions are made evenly through the year and released at the end of the year you buy.
  • When released, the 85% and the earnings are taxed at your marginal rate (with the Medicare levy), less a 30% offset. Your marginal rate is worked out on your salary in the year you buy, grown by your pay rise.
  • The bank comparison saves the same drop in take-home pay each year, with interest taxed at your marginal rate.
  • Contribution limits and employer super use your current salary every year.

What it leaves out, and what that means

  • After-tax contributions. Personal contributions you don’t claim a deduction for can also count, and 100% of them can be released. They aren’t included.
  • Amounts you’ve already contributed. They reduce the limits left, but what they’d add to your deposit isn’t included.
  • Carry-forward of unused cap. If your super balance is under $500,000, you may be able to contribute more before tax using unused cap from earlier years. The calculator uses the $32,500 cap each year.
  • Other tax. Division 293 tax (for incomes over $250,000), HELP repayments, low-income offsets, the Medicare levy surcharge and your other income aren’t included. The released amount could also push some of your income into a higher tax bracket.
  • Changes to tax rates and caps. 2026–27 tax rates and caps are used for every year. The 15% tax rate is legislated to fall to 14% from 1 July 2027, and caps rise with wages from time to time.
  • Fees, insurance and investment returns. FHSS earnings are worked out by the ATO at a set rate, whatever your super fund earns, so fund fees and returns don’t change the amount released. They do change your super balance.
  • Eligibility. You must meet the scheme’s conditions, and you must ask the ATO for an FHSS determination before you sign a contract to buy or build, or within 14 days of signing.

Assumptions, and why we use them

FHSS earnings rate 7.51% a year · you can change it
The ATO works out associated earnings at the shortfall interest charge rate, which it updates each quarter. 7.51% is the rate for October to December 2026. Future rates may be higher or lower.
Bank interest rate 4.5% a year · you can change it
A rate a high-interest savings account might pay. Interest is taxed at your marginal rate each year.
Inflation, for today’s dollars 2.5% a year · you can change it
Results are in future dollars. Each is also shown in today’s dollars, discounted by this rate for the years until you buy. 2.5% is the middle of the Reserve Bank of Australia’s 2–3% target, and ASIC’s default for calculators.
Employer super rate 12% · you can change it
The super guarantee rate since 1 July 2025. Some employers pay more.
Pay rise 3% a year · you can change it
A common rate of wage growth. It’s only used to estimate your salary, and so your tax rate, in the year you withdraw.
Limits and tax rates Set by law
The $32,500 before-tax cap, $15,000 yearly and $50,000 total FHSS limits, 85% release, 30% offset, 2026–27 resident tax rates and the 2% Medicare levy, applied in full.
